# Data, Leakage, Survey-Design, and Applicability Audit

**Version:** 0.1, 23 August 2026

**Evidence tier:** Development audit

**Final-outcome access:** Not authorized

## 1. Executive finding

The configured cohorts load reproducibly and the existing development split
manifests pass their structural contracts. The project nevertheless has two
pre-final design gaps:

1. BRFSS survey weights, strata, and PSUs are available but are not used by the
   current prediction or uncertainty pipeline.
2. Ordinary split manifests contain labels and their metadata reports partition
   event counts/prevalence, so they cannot act as concealed final manifests.

These gaps do not invalidate development work. They must be resolved before any
population-representative BRFSS language or fresh final holdout is permitted.

## 3. BRFSS complex survey design

The raw retained cohort has complete values for `_LLCPWT`, `_STSTR`, and `_PSU`.
It contains 53 state/territory codes, 1,306 sampling strata, and 23,081 PSUs.
The CDC documentation specifies `_LLCPWT` as the final core weight, `_STSTR` for
stratification, and `_PSU` for clustering in complex-survey analyses:

- <https://www.cdc.gov/brfss/annual_data/annual_2015.html>
- <https://www.cdc.gov/brfss/annual_data/2015/pdf/overview_2015.pdf>
- <https://www.cdc.gov/brfss/data_documentation/pdf/UserguideJune2013.pdf>

The configured outcome prevalence is 13.34% unweighted and 10.78% under a simple
`_LLCPWT` weighted calculation. The weighted point estimate does not itself provide
a design-correct standard error.

### Decision

- Primary prediction estimates may remain explicitly sample-conditional if that
  target is justified.
- Weighted cohort description is mandatory.
- A survey-aware sensitivity analysis must evaluate weighted training and/or
  weighted held-out performance and design-correct uncertainty where technically
  supported.
- Population-representative claims are prohibited until that analysis is reviewed.
- Subgroup/fairness results must disclose whether they are sample-conditional or survey-weighted.

## 4. Missingness

### PIDD

Physiological zero codes are converted to missing without using the outcome. The
largest missingness rates are insulin and skin thickness. Missingness is somewhat
higher among positive cases for skin thickness and insulin; fold-fitted median
imputation with a missingness indicator is therefore retained and must be ablated.

### Sylhet

No configured predictor is missing. The primary threat is duplicate-profile
leakage, not imputation.

### BRFSS

Missingness is substantial and outcome-dependent for some variables. High
cholesterol has the largest differential because questionnaire eligibility and
response patterns differ between outcome groups. Numeric missingness indicators
are currently added inside the fold-fitted preprocessor. The manuscript must
describe these indicators as measurement/response-pattern information, not
biological risk factors.

### Hospital130

Every encounter misses at least one configured field. `max_glu_serum`, `A1Cresult`,
medical specialty, payer code, and race have the largest missingness. Missingness
may encode care processes and must not be interpreted causally.

## 5. Duplicate and grouping control

- PIDD contains no exact configured-feature duplicates.
- Sylhet contains 269 duplicate rows across 251 unique profiles. All identical
  profiles have consistent outcomes. Profile grouping is mandatory in outer and
  inner validation.
- BRFSS exact configured-feature duplicates exist but do not represent known
  repeated respondents; the source-row identifier remains the evaluation unit.
- Hospital130 has 16,341 repeated-patient groups containing 45,694 encounters.
  All encounters from a patient must remain in one partition, and uncertainty
  must resample complete patients.

## 6. Split-manifest control

All seed-42 manifests pass the configured stratification/grouping validations.
They are development-only because:

- their CSV files contain the target column;
- their JSON metadata reports event counts and prevalence by partition;
- BRFSS and Hospital130 smoke runs already evaluated their test roles.

A final manifest must use a separate prespecified seed, omit outcome fields from
the persisted public manifest and metadata, be checksum-locked, and be consumed
only after a signed authorization. Development code must be unable to regenerate
or summarize the final partition.

## 7. Endpoint and applicability control

- PIDD, Sylhet, and BRFSS estimate different recorded diabetes-status labels and
  remain separate analyses.
- BRFSS is cross-sectional and self-reported; it does not estimate future incidence.
- Hospital130 predicts 30-day readmission and is excluded from diabetes-detection efficacy claims.
- Mixed-source routing is a software demonstration. Several eligible cohort
  experts trigger abstention unless the environment is explicitly selected.
- No current dataset can support age-of-onset prediction.
